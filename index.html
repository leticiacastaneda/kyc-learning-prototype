Deliverable 1 · Learning & change recommendation

The client asked for training. What they need is adoption.

Six months after launch, people know the process exists. Here is why it isn't used consistently, and a 90-day plan to change that, with training as one part of it.

What we assume about the client (open each to see why it matters)

The platform records each step of a file

Why it matters: it lets us track weekly whether the full process is followed. If not: we use quality sampling and manager file reviews instead.

Quality data can be split by step, team and site

Why it matters: it shows where errors concentrate and which teams already do it well. If not: a sample audit builds the baseline, and results take longer to show.

The old method can still be used outside the platform

Why it matters: it explains how experts revert. If not: workarounds happen inside the platform, and we track them there.

Compliance can review new content within an agreed time

Why it matters: guides and cases can't go live without approval. If not: we narrow the scope to the most critical steps.

Experienced staff can dedicate some time to support peers

Why it matters: they verify colleagues on the job. If not: managers take that role, and progress is slower.

3 · What the evidence says

Five lessons from organizations that faced the same problem

Different industries, same pattern: a correct process that people did not follow consistently. Each lesson shows up in the plan.

1
Fix the system before the course

Harvard research on corporate training: people trained and sent back to an unchanged system don't apply what they learned.

In the plan: managers and guides change before training starts
2
Put the help where the work happens

A one-page surgical checklist in 8 hospitals across 8 countries cut major complications from 11% to 7%.

In the plan: one-page step guides inside the workflow
3
Learn from the teams that already succeed

U.S. veterans' hospitals let frontline staff design and teach the practices: ICU infections fell 62%.

In the plan: expert referents build the practice cases
4
Treat resistance as information

Research on resistance to change: the loudest critics often see real flaws in the plan.

In the plan: a no-blame friction channel; experts join the diagnosis
5
Leaders decide whether a change sticks

16 hospitals learned the same new surgery. By the 40th case, the fastest team took 143 minutes and the slowest 305: how leaders framed the change explained the gap.

In the plan: managers calibrated first, then a weekly review

5 · How we'll measure it

Three questions, answered over the same 90 days

By day 90: analysts master the 2–3 most critical steps, managers reinforce one shared standard every week, and first-time-right files on those steps improve against today's starting point.

Before changing anything, we take a picture of today. Then we answer three questions in order: if people don't know how, they can't do it; if they don't do it, quality can't improve.

1

Do they know how?

Every analyst solves real-looking files and says how sure they are. We compare the first assessment with the one after training, step by step.

Week 3: before trainingWeek 10: after training

Good at day 90: most analysts master the critical steps; no confident wrong answers left on them.

2

Do they do it on the job?

Each week we look at how many files follow the full process (always together with their quality), how many reversions are reported, and whether managers held their file review. Managers repeat the calibration to check they still agree.

Weeks 2 and 7: managers comparedWeekly from week 5

Good at day 90: the full process is the norm on critical steps, and every team holds its weekly review.

3

Does quality improve?

We compare files that pass Quality the first time, and the gap between the best and worst teams, against the starting point.

Weeks 1–3: starting pointDay 90: compared

Good at day 90: a clear early improvement on the critical steps. Fewer audit findings come after the project.

Process adherence is always read together with qualityNo penalties during the 90 daysTargets agreed with the client

Measurement draws on documented frameworks: Kirkpatrick's four levels of evaluation (learning, behavior, results), Prosci's adoption factors (speed, utilization and proficiency) and the ADKAR assessment to diagnose where each group is stuck.

2 · What learners will be able to do

Find the mismatch, then choose the right next step

Given a client file, the analyst identifies inconsistencies between the identity documents and the data captured in the platform (name, ID number, date of birth, address and document validity) and chooses the right next action before submitting it.

WhoAnalysts whose assessment showed gaps in this step

FormatSelf-paced online, with real-looking sample files

3 · How it's built

From watching to doing it alone, with less help at each stage

Learners first see why it matters and how it's done. Then they practice, and the help fades step by step until they can do it on their own, just like on the job. The step guide stays open the whole time, because it is the same support they will use at work.

5 · Designed for everyone

Assessment, accessibility and media by design, not as an add-on

Assessment

Instant feedback while practicing. In the check, learners also say how sure they are, so we catch confident mistakes. Results are shown by check, never as one score, and never used in performance reviews.

Accessibility · WCAG 2.2 AA

Every click-on-image task has a keyboard and list alternative. Alt text, captions, no color-only cues, AA contrast, no time limits, works at 200% zoom and on mobile.

Visual & media

Dark title screens, light reading screens. Mock platform and documents marked “Sample”. Illustrations instead of photos, and one optional audio tip.

3 · How we handle resistance

Resistance is information: a loop, not a fight

When someone goes back to the old way, they are telling us something about the person, the tool or the process. The plan runs this loop from week 1 to day 90.

1ListenFriction channel, separate group interviews, reversions tracked by step
2Find the causeIs the gap in the person, the tool or the process?
Resistance
is information
3Act on that causePractice for a skill gap, a better guide for a tool gap, a fixed step for a process flaw
4Report backTell people what we heard and what changed, so speaking up pays off
Example from the plan

Experts say the new way “takes longer” across many steps → the cause is fluency, not attitude → targeted practice and step guides, decided at the week-3 decision gate. If instead reversions cluster on one step with a real flaw, the step itself is fixed with the process owner.

3 · Where my judgment changed the work

Three moments that shaped the final result

1
“Where did this come from?”

I kept asking for the origin of claims. AI acknowledged that three design rationales and a reassessment in week 10 were its own additions, never discussed. I rewrote the first and kept the second consciously, knowing why it matters.

2
“Is this true?”

I asked for every case to be checked against its original study. That caught a wrong figure and separated peer-reviewed evidence from vendor stories, which are labeled as illustrative.

3
“Will an evaluator understand this?”

Reading the first drafts as an evaluator would, I found too much text and too little story. I redirected the whole format into a narrative, chapter by chapter, with detail only on demand.
